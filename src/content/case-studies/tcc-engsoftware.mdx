## Overview

This is my undergraduate thesis in Software Engineering at UEPG. It builds and evaluates three deep-learning architectures — a 1D CNN, a stacked LSTM and a hybrid CNN+LSTM — to predict the next closing price of two Brazilian mini futures contracts, the mini dollar (WDO) and the mini index (WIN), from one-hour (H1) candles.

Beyond the models themselves, the work is organized as a reproducible experiment: every scenario I tested has its own script, every model generation is saved side by side, and every result is evaluated both with error metrics and with a trading backtest that includes transaction costs and significance tests.

## Problem and goals

Price prediction papers often report low error on a test set and stop there. A low RMSE on prices does not mean a model knows which way the market will move, and it says nothing about whether acting on its predictions would make money after costs. The goals were to:

- compare CNN, LSTM and hybrid architectures on the same data and the same preprocessing;
- evaluate them on **direction**, not only on price error;
- test whether any combination produces a trading edge that survives transaction costs **and** a statistical significance test;
- keep every experiment reproducible from a single command.

## Implementation

The models predict the **log return** of the next candle; the price is reconstructed afterwards from the last known close. Prices are converted to log returns before scaling:

```python
def prepare_features(df):
    df = df.copy()
    for col in PRICE_COLS:
        df[col] = np.log(df[col] / df[col].shift(1))
    df = df.iloc[1:].reset_index(drop=True)
    return df
```

The data is split chronologically into 70% training, 15% validation and 15% test, and the scaler is fitted **on the training slice only**, so nothing from the future leaks into training:

```python
df_train, df_val, df_test = split_chronological(df)

scaler = MinMaxScaler(feature_range=(0, 1))
train_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(df_train.values)
val_scaled = scaler.transform(df_val.values)
test_scaled = scaler.transform(df_test.values)
```

Each of the six combinations (2 markets × 3 architectures) trains for up to 200 epochs with early stopping (patience 12). Trained models, scalers and metrics are saved per **generation** — `_log`, `_tuned`, `_features` — so later experiments never overwrite earlier ones and every comparison stays reproducible.

## Technical decisions

**Log returns instead of absolute prices.** The first version scaled raw prices. For a strongly trending series such as WIN, test-period prices can sit far above anything seen in training, so a Min-Max scaler fitted on training data pushes the model to extrapolate outside its domain. Log returns are stationary — their distribution does not shift with the price level — which removes the problem regardless of how far the price moves.

**Evaluate on direction and money, not just error.** Price RMSE is reported, but the decisive metrics are directional accuracy and a backtest: two strategies (single-bar and multi-bar holding) with a fixed round-trip transaction cost per market, an annualized Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, historical VaR at 95%, and a one-sample t-test on per-trade returns.

**One script per scenario.** Each experiment in `src/pipelines/` documents its motivation, flow, produced artifacts and reference result in its docstring, and can be rerun with `python -m src.pipelines.<name>`.

**Correct for multiple comparisons.** The final scenario ran 216 hypothesis tests. At p < 0.05, about 10.8 false positives are expected by chance alone, so results were also checked against a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (0.05 / 216 ≈ 0.000231).

## Testing and validation

Six scenarios were run in sequence, each building on what the previous one showed:

| #   | Scenario                                                   | Reported result                                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Baseline (`_log`, 5 log-return features)                   | Directional accuracy ~0.49–0.50 for all six models; Sharpe negative in all 12 combinations, 10 of 12 statistically significant — consistent losses, not an edge       |
| 2   | Confidence filter by predicted-return magnitude            | 7 of 72 combinations with positive Sharpe, all with p > 0.4; all 41 significant combinations had negative Sharpe                                                      |
| 3   | Ensemble agreement (all three architectures agree)         | All 4 combinations negative, 3 of 4 significant (p < 0.001); agreement rate 60–82% of windows                                                                         |
| 4   | Hyperparameter search (Keras Tuner, `_tuned`)              | Directional accuracy ~0.49–0.50; Sharpe negative in 12 of 12, 11 significant                                                                                          |
| 5   | Technical indicators (RSI, SMA, MACD, `_features`)         | Directional accuracy ~0.49–0.50; Sharpe negative in 12 of 12, 11 significant                                                                                          |
| 6   | Confidence filter on `_tuned` and `_features` + Bonferroni | 3 combinations had positive Sharpe (+10.5 to +12.3) with p between 0.017 and 0.041, all at the most extreme filter (88 trades); none survives the corrected threshold |

<Callout kind="note">
  The honest conclusion is a negative one: no combination of architecture,
  hyperparameters, feature set or trade-selection rule produced a statistically
  defensible directional edge for WIN or WDO at the one-hour horizon. The value
  of the project is the method that makes this conclusion trustworthy.
</Callout>

## Limitations and future work

- The repository has no automated test suite, linter or formatter; validation happens through the scenario scripts and their reported metrics.
- The detailed rationale for each decision lives in a personal notes file that is not versioned, so the public repository only carries the summaries in docstrings and the thesis PDF.
- Only the one-hour horizon and two contracts were tested, and transaction costs are approximated as a fixed round-trip value per market.
- Re-extracting data requires a MetaTrader 5 terminal reachable through a local bridge; the committed CSVs are needed to reproduce results without it.

Natural next steps would be adding tests around the data-preparation and backtest functions, and evaluating other horizons with the same statistical safeguards.
